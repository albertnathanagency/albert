Children's Fiction

Kate Cullen

Game On Boys! The PlayStation Play-offs
(Game on Boys Series, Book 1)
Campaign Type Multi-Channel Book Launch Services Used Amazon, Social, Outreach, Ads Campaign Duration 8 Weeks
The Author and The Book

Kate Cullen is the author of the Game on Boys! series. A children's fiction series built around the world of gaming, friendship, and competitive play that young readers aged 8 to 13 immediately connect with. The first title, The PlayStation Play-offs, had strong creative foundations. A relatable protagonist, a world kids live in, and writing that captured the excitement of gaming without losing the heart of a coming-of-age story.

The challenge was that none of that would matter if the book could not be found by the parents and children it was written for.

The Challenge

When Kate came to Albert Nathan Agency, her book was published and available. But its online visibility was limited. The Amazon listing was not optimized for the searches parents and children were actually making. There was no coordinated marketing campaign behind the release. The book was not reaching the gaming-focused family audience that would love it most, and social proof in the form of reviews was building slowly.

The core problem: a great book for a very specific, passionate audience. With no clear marketing pathway to that audience.

The Strategy

Albert Nathan Agency designed a campaign around the book's core audience: parents of children aged 8 to 13 who engaged with gaming content online, and children themselves who were active in gaming communities and on social platforms.

The strategy was built in three coordinated layers. Platform visibility, Amazon optimization and discovery. Targeted audience reach, social media advertising directed at gaming families. And community outreach, influencer and blogger engagement in the children's book and gaming space.

Rather than promoting the book generically, every piece of marketing was positioned around the specific emotional hook that made the book unique. The thrill of competition, the loyalty of friendship, and the world every gaming kid already lived in.

Services Deployed in This Campaign
Amazon Listing Optimization Amazon Ads Facebook and Instagram Ads Influencer Outreach Book Blogger Outreach Goodreads Promotion Reader Community Engagement Review Campaign Social Media Strategy
Campaign Execution

The campaign began with a full audit of the existing Amazon listing. Rewriting the book description with targeted keywords, selecting optimal categories, and restructuring the presentation to convert browsers into buyers more effectively. Amazon Sponsored Products campaigns were launched targeting relevant children's fiction categories and competitor titles.

Simultaneously, Facebook and Instagram advertising campaigns were built targeting parents of school-age children who engaged with gaming content, children's book pages, and family entertainment. Ad creative was developed around the book's core appeal, with multiple variations tested and optimized based on click-through and conversion data.

Outreach to children's book bloggers, family content influencers, and gaming-adjacent content creators was coordinated to build organic coverage, reader reviews, and social proof. Layering credibility on top of the paid visibility the advertising was building.

Literary Fiction

Diana Setterfield

Once Upon a River: A Novel
Campaign Type Pre-Launch and Launch Campaign Services Used Goodreads, Social Ads, Outreach Campaign Duration 10 Weeks
The Author and The Book

Diana Setterfield is the author of literary fiction that operates at the intersection of the gothic, the atmospheric, and the deeply human. Once Upon a River, a novel of mystery, myth, and river-world England, arrived with the weight of a highly anticipated literary event behind it. Building on the reputation established by her celebrated debut.

The book had a dedicated existing readership and critical credibility. The marketing challenge was not starting from scratch. It was capitalizing on the momentum of anticipation while simultaneously breaking into new reader audiences who had not yet encountered her work.

The Challenge

Literary fiction sits in a competitive, crowded market where discoverability is driven by word-of-mouth, critical recommendation, and community engagement more than traditional advertising alone. The campaign needed to activate the existing fanbase, build anticipation among new readers, and ensure that the book's launch arrived at peak visibility. Not as an afterthought once reviews trickled in.

The specific challenge: reaching literary fiction readers who did not yet know Diana Setterfield's name while simultaneously energizing the readers who had been waiting for the next book.

The Strategy

Albert Nathan Agency built the campaign in two distinct but coordinated phases. A pre-launch phase designed to build anticipation and Goodreads momentum, and a launch phase designed to maximize visibility at the moment of publication.

The pre-launch campaign focused on Goodreads reader community activation. Building "want to read" momentum among literary fiction readers, coordinating with book bloggers and literary review platforms to build early coverage, and developing a social media content strategy that built atmospheric anticipation aligned with the tone of the novel itself.

The launch phase activated Facebook and Instagram advertising campaigns targeting literary fiction readers, book club members, and readers of comparable titles. With retargeting sequences that converted awareness into purchase intent at the critical launch window.

Services Deployed in This Campaign
Goodreads Promotion Book Blogger Outreach Facebook and Instagram Ads Social Media Strategy Literary Influencer Outreach Pre-Launch Campaign Launch Day Campaign Email Campaign Support Campaign Analytics
Campaign Execution

The Goodreads campaign began six weeks before publication. Building reader shelving activity, coordinating with literary bloggers for early review placements, and seeding the book into reading communities where literary fiction with atmospheric, gothic qualities had established readership.

A social media content strategy was developed that leaned into the visual and tonal world of the novel. River imagery, winter atmosphere, mystery. Building organic anticipation through thematic content rather than straightforward promotional posts. This created a pre-publication social presence that felt like a natural extension of the book's world.

Facebook and Instagram advertising campaigns launched in the final two weeks before publication, targeting literary fiction readers and book club communities with audiences built around comparable titles and reader interest categories. The launch week campaign concentrated maximum advertising investment at the point of peak reader interest, with retargeting deployed to convert those who had engaged with pre-launch content.

How We Define Success

What Every Albert Nathan Agency Campaign Measures

Results mean different things to different authors. Every campaign is built around the metrics that matter most for the specific book, objective, and audience.

Book Sales and Revenue

Where measurable and attributable, campaign-driven sales uplift. Including launch week sales, promotional period spikes, and sustained monthly sales growth.

Amazon Rankings

Category rankings, bestseller status, and organic search position across Amazon's book marketplace. Key indicators of platform visibility and commercial momentum.

Audience and Reader Growth

Social media following growth, email list growth, Goodreads reader additions, and community engagement growth. Building the long-term author platform.

Reviews and Social Proof

The number and quality of Amazon and Goodreads reviews generated through the campaign. The social proof that drives organic book discovery long after a campaign ends.

Advertising Performance

Click-through rates, cost-per-click, return on ad spend (ROAS), and impression volume across Amazon, Meta, and TikTok advertising campaigns.

Reach and Visibility

Total campaign reach across all channels. The number of unique people who encountered the book through campaign activity, from advertising impressions to influencer mentions.
